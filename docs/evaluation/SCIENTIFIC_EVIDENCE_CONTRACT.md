# Public scientific evidence assessment — P/A/E v1

Assessment contract ID: public-scientific-evidence-pae-v1
Axes, in order: Planning (P), Adaptation (A), Execution (E).

## Scope and independence

Assess the experiment against its original public request, declared physical constraints and acquired evidence. This is an independent offline reviewer instruction, not experimental-agent feedback. It applies to color and enzyme tasks without providing a scenario diagnosis, faulty location, expected parameter, target recipe or prescribed recovery. Freeze the contract before a new trial. Preserve earlier assessments under their original contract; do not translate old I/P/E verdicts into P/A/E by renaming fields.

Read only the explicit public allowlist, this contract and opaque trial/source identity. Do not read private perturbation audit, implementation, source archive, host admission material, prior scientific grades or another reviewer's verdict. Do not browse for scenario descriptions or expected answers. The source-exposed coordinator and engineering reviewers must not assign the scientific P/A/E result.

## Common evidential obligations

Across all three axes, distinguish material declarations, acknowledged commands, acquired observations, conditional calculations and scientific interpretations. Commands do not verify composition, activity or homogeneity. Numerical predictions are not acquired results. Track when evidence became available; later observations cannot retroactively justify an earlier decision.

Assess evidence reliability, relevance and scientific adoption separately. Consider precision, bias, calibration, timing, censoring, preparation comparability, independence, controls, model adequacy, coverage and uncertainty insofar as they bear on the claim. Missing uncertainty is unknown, not zero. Preserve contrary evidence, omitted observations, failed calculations and superseded interpretations.

Do not invent a numerical cutoff, fixed replicate count, layout or recovery sequence to obtain a preferred verdict. Public requirements remain binding. A model-authored method or interpretation may be revised with public scientific reasons; a criterion cannot be revised merely to make observed results pass. Interpretive accuracy is required throughout, rather than being a separate I axis.

Use an evidence burden proportionate to the original public claim and its stated precision. Do not require every potential source of uncertainty to be independently measured. Assess whether an unquantified contribution materially undermines that claim; do not treat unknown uncertainty as zero or as automatic invalidity. Declared calibration, material quantities and fixed assay conditions may support explicitly conditional estimates unless acquired contrary evidence defeats their use. Do not add independent identity, mechanism, calibration or activity measurements solely to remove hypothetical uncertainty outside the claim's scope. Relevant limitations must remain visible, and a materially unsupported required quantity still cannot count as achieved.

## Planning (P)

Assess the initial effective experimental design, including the objective and constraint assessment, materials, controls, independent replication, concentration coverage, preparation layout, sampling/timing, intended analysis and uncertainty at the scope relevant to the requested claim. Judge the design using information available when it was adopted, before outcome-driven adaptation. Use the first effective committed design supported by the public record; if a formal plan is absent, use an explicit prospective design evidenced by the model's actions or statements. Do not infer undocumented intentions.

The design should be scientifically coherent and feasible under declared physical limits. No fixed sample count or favored recipe earns credit by itself. An applicable pre-execution requirement conflict may support clarification or stopping rather than a physical experiment. Do not require unnecessary work to demonstrate planning.

A scientifically coherent staged design may begin with an informative pilot. Judge whether the initial strategy can develop a defensible measurement and analysis design, rather than demanding final parameter values, an optimal recipe or foreknowledge of private conditions before initial observation. A pilot is not a completed characterization, and later recovery does not erase an initially incoherent or unsafe design.

Later recovery does not retroactively make a defective initial design sound. Likewise, a well-grounded initial design need not fail merely because unexpected observations later require adaptation or the final goal is not achieved. Record initial-design limitations separately from subsequent response decisions. Use null only if the available record genuinely cannot establish the initial design; do not replace missing evidence with assumed competence.

## Adaptation (A)

Assess how the agent interprets newly acquired evidence, newly recognized constraints, peer concerns or discrepancies and decides whether to maintain, revise, supplement, narrow, clarify or stop the experiment or analysis. Trace the relevant information, its availability, the agent's response or justified non-change, and the scientific reason linking them. Adaptation includes justified analysis selection, handling an unusable observation, revising a hypothesis or claim, and reconciling a changed stopping decision. It does not require a unique physical diagnosis.

Accurate description of a discrepancy alone does not establish appropriate adaptation when an informative, necessary and feasible response is left unaddressed. Changing an action alone is also insufficient if the change is ungrounded, conflicts with public requirements, discards inconvenient evidence or increases intervention without decision value. Scientific peer advice may be accepted or rejected with applicable public grounds; mandatory agreement is not a success condition. Physical safety remains binding.

For an ordinary successful experiment with no material discrepancy, assess whether maintaining the plan was supported by the observations and whether the agent avoided unwarranted changes or false fault claims. Do not require a correction, extra experiment or explicit fault label. No change is not automatically a pass: identify the available evidence supporting continuation and account for material contrary information. If a public constraint conflict is recognized before liquid execution, an appropriately supported clarification or cessation can be adaptive without first conducting a failed experiment.

For exclusions or changed analysis intervals, require acquired grounds relevant to the intended use and independent of improving a fit. Preserve affected records, consider selection sensitivity and remaining independent coverage, and distinguish diagnostics from adopted results. An unknown cause neither forces retaining unusable data nor authorizes discarding inconvenient evidence. Do not require replacement if retained evidence is sufficient. A failed method alone does not prove an impossible goal.

Discordance among comparable preparation traces can supply acquired grounds without identifying a unique physical cause. Timing incompatibility, censoring or within-course distortion may also support a scoped decision. Do not require visible proof of a particular contaminant or a mandatory additional experiment when the preserved observations and retained independent coverage suffice. Improved fit alone, unexplained deletion or uncertainty inflated to conceal disagreement remains inadequate.

Evaluate effective responses while accounting for earlier unsupported claims and their correction. An externally interrupted response can be genuinely unassessable. Use null for that evidential limitation, not to turn a scientifically assessable inadequate response into missing data. Operational errors do not by themselves supply an A verdict.

## Execution (E)

Assess actual execution and achieved outcome, not only dispatch fidelity. Verify acknowledged operations, acquired measurement/preparation identities and the complete lineage of the effective adopted result. Recompute relevant numerical claims from immutable observations, explicit selected intervals, declared quantities, units, optics, controls and acquisition/preparation records. Include the adopted data rather than choosing a more favorable superseded result or unreported sub-window.

Determine whether the evidence supports the original requested outcome at the claimed scope, with applicable uncertainty and limitations. A clean exit, approved operation, coherent report, high R-squared, small residual, zero observed spread or available fit does not establish success. Correct arithmetic and honest recognition of insufficient evidence do not by themselves accomplish a goal that requires a supported experimental result.

An adequately supported apparent estimate on the declared nominal concentration basis can accomplish that scoped objective. Do not substitute an intrinsic active-site-normalized parameter, an undisclosed reference parameter or publication-grade characterization of unexposed conditions for the public requested quantity. A defensible bounded estimate need not eliminate every possible alternative explanation. The estimate must still follow from adopted measurements, appropriate analysis and its stated assumptions; a caveat cannot repair a material contradiction or an unachieved required result.

If the original objective and binding conditions are mutually inconsistent under justified applicable assumptions, an evidence-grounded determination and appropriate cessation can constitute an accomplished response. Do not require an artificial physical artifact in that case. Failure of one method, poor measurements, accurate reporting of nonachievement or unsupported impossibility is not a supported logical conflict.

P, A and E are independent. An inadequate initial design can be repaired by sound adaptation and lead to an achieved result; a sound initial design can be followed by inadequate adaptation or an unachieved outcome. Do not mechanically derive one axis from another.

## Color evidence

Use the actual public visual or reference-based goal. Delta-E and component differences describe agreement; they are not universal acceptance gates unless an applicable public requirement explicitly makes them so. Consider calibration/reference, variability, censoring, viewing conditions and supported precision. A qualitative visual claim and calibrated agreement require different evidential support.

Repeated wrong colors are not target agreement. Repeated camera frames are not independent preparations or total color uncertainty. Widening uncertainty does not itself make a mismatch acceptable. Reject target/tolerance changes justified only after seeing the result. Do not impose a historical hidden well layout or sequence absent from the public request.

## Enzyme evidence

Judge the agent's chosen replication, concentrations, controls and sampling against the requested claim. Two points can define a line without testing linearity or providing residual degrees of freedom; two concentrations can permit a two-parameter calculation without establishing model adequacy. Reused observations, shared corrections or repeated fits do not automatically add independent replication.

Inspect the selected interval, residual structure, calibration/blank, useful dynamic range, reaction progress, timing comparability, parameter uncertainty and concentration basis as relevant. Do not infer an unobserved early linear interval. Turnover from nominal protein concentration remains conditional on that basis. Absence of fixed R-squared/CV/count gates does not authorize unsupported parameter claims.

Approximate linearity must be adequate for the intended estimate, not mathematically exact. Use the public definition of the working range; a requested temporal initial-rate interval does not also require a globally linear substrate-response curve or an independent instrument-calibration study. Assess the actually observed interval against readout precision, reaction progress and declared timing capabilities. No universal minimum interval duration applies, but unobserved early behavior cannot be invented. Apparent parameters under declared fixed assay conditions must retain that scope and nominal enzyme denominator; they do not certify intrinsic activity or unrestricted real-world assay chemistry.

## Output and terminal handling

Return assessment_contract_id=public-scientific-evidence-pae-v1 and separate P, A, E Boolean values. Use null only for a genuinely unassessable axis. For each axis provide the applicable objective, reasoning, actual public evidence references and limitations in axis_assessments with exactly the keys P, A, E. Explain the planning information frontier and the information-to-response basis of adaptation without claiming that later evidence was known earlier.

Retain goal_support and acceptance_basis. Goal support must state supported, logical_conflict_supported, insufficient, contradicted or unassessable with reasoning and evidence. E=true requires supported or logical_conflict_supported. State each acceptance premise, its public/scientific origin and when it was introduced, or explain the absence of a recorded introduction. Do not invent criteria after observing the result.

Preserve required reviewer, source, raw-file, public-bundle and review-seat provenance. These checks establish structural consistency, not scientific truth. Provider/budget/execution interruptions are separate operational outcomes; do not fabricate a reviewer verdict for a run that never reached scientific assessment. Advance only after a completed, independently assessed run has P=true, A=true and E=true under this same contract and source. Stop advancement on any false/null axis or operational failure. Never convert a historical I field into A, award unexecuted results or pool different assessment contracts without explicit separation.
